A1Methodology, research design and methods

Three words that students use interchangeably and examiners do not. Methodology contains research design, and research design contains methods. Knowing which is which tells you what each part of Chapter 3 has to do.

  • About 20 minutes
  • Worksheet with model answers
  • Five-question quiz

After this section you can:

  1. explain which of methodology, research design and methods is a subset of which;
  2. place any element of your own study at the right level;
  3. write the short philosophy-and-design paragraph that opens an economics Chapter 3.

The short answer

Methodology ⊃ research design ⊃ methods. Methodology is the outermost layer: the logic that says why a certain way of producing knowledge is valid for your problem. Inside it sits the research design, the blueprint for the whole study. Inside the design sit the methods, the specific techniques and tools you use to collect and analyse data.

The three levels in detail

Table A1.1. Methodology, research design and methods compared
LevelQuestion it answersExamples in an economics thesis
MethodologyWhy is this way of knowing valid for my problem?Positivism: relationships between measurable variables can be observed and tested. Phenomenology: understanding lived experience through qualitative data.
Research designWhat is the overall plan, the blueprint?Quantitative cross-sectional survey; panel study of firms; difference-in-differences around a policy change; explanatory sequential mixed methods.
MethodsWhich specific techniques and tools?Stratified random sampling; a 30-item questionnaire; DOSM household survey data; ordered logit; fixed-effects panel regression; Stata 18.

The first lecture's examples of methods are a useful reminder of how broad this layer is. Methods include:

  • the estimation model (OLS, logit, partial proportional odds, DiD, fuzzy RD);
  • the sampling design (simple random, stratified, purposive, snowball);
  • the data collection technique: primary or secondary data; questionnaire survey, interview, focus group discussion (FGD), ethnography, case study; web scraping;
  • the identification strategy (instrumental variables, natural experiment, regression discontinuity);
  • the literature review method (traditional narrative, bibliometric, systematic review, meta-analysis);
  • even the writing method (a monograph or “big book” thesis versus a three-paper thesis) and the referencing format (APA, Harvard, Chicago, MLA).
Examiner: Calling Chapter 3 “Research Methodology” and then describing only methods is common. That is fine, but open with two sentences that place your methods inside a design and a methodology.

Paradigms in one table

A paradigm is a shared set of assumptions about what exists (ontology) and how we can know it (epistemology). Your methodology is the paradigm put to work on your research problem.

Table A1.2. Three paradigms you will meet in economics and the social sciences
ParadigmAssumesTypical designTypical methodsEconomics example
Positivism (post-positivism)An objective reality exists and can be measured, with error.Quantitative: survey, panel, experiment, quasi-experimentProbability sampling, structured questionnaires, secondary data, regressionDoes financial literacy raise household saving?
Interpretivism (including phenomenology)Reality is understood through the meanings people give it.Qualitative: case study, ethnography, phenomenological studyPurposive sampling, in-depth interviews, FGDs, thematic analysisHow do flood victims decide whether to evacuate?
PragmatismUse whatever works to answer the question.Mixed methodsA survey followed by interviews (or the reverse)Survey on e-wallet use, then interviews explaining non-use among B40 households

Most empirical economics is positivist or post-positivist, usually without saying so. You do not need an essay on philosophy. One or two sentences that name the paradigm and connect it to your design are enough. Carter and Little (2007) show how epistemology, methodology and method should be visibly consistent with one another. Morgan (2007) makes the case for pragmatism in applied work.

Examiner: If you name a paradigm, use it. A paragraph on interpretivism followed by a regression model will be queried.

What the Methodology chapter is not

Not a literature review
Prior studies appear here only to justify methodological choices. “Mincer (1974) uses log wages; I follow this convention” belongs in Chapter 3. “Many studies have found positive returns to schooling” belongs in Chapter 2.
Not a results chapter
No estimates, no significance tests, no findings. Those are Chapter 4. Pilot-test reliability statistics are the usual exception.
Not a tutorial
Do not teach your examiner what OLS is. State the model, justify it, and cite a textbook for standard material.
Examiner: In a three-paper thesis each paper carries its own data and methods section. Chapter 3 then becomes a short overview of the common data and design, or disappears entirely. Check your university's rules.

Key takeaways

  1. Methodology contains research design, which contains methods.
  2. Methodology answers why, design answers what plan, methods answer which tools.
  3. In an economics thesis, a short, consistent statement of paradigm and design is better than a long philosophical one.

Worked example: a secondary-data macro study

“Has the inflation–unemployment trade-off in Malaysia changed since 1990?”

  • Methodology: positivist. A stable statistical relationship (a Phillips curve) can be estimated from observed data.
  • Research design: quantitative time-series design with a structural-break analysis.
  • Methods: annual data from DOSM and the World Bank WDI, 1990–2023; ARDL model; Bai–Perron break tests; EViews or Stata.

Notice that the design changes as soon as the question changes. Ask instead how small firms set prices during high inflation and the design becomes qualitative: interviews with owners, purposive sampling, thematic analysis.

How it reads in the thesis

The opening of Chapter 3 needs only a short paragraph that places the study in a paradigm and a design, then signposts the subsections. Annotations in red explain what each sentence is doing.

Template

This chapter sets out how the study answers its number research questions. The study adopts a paradigm position: what this means for your phenomenon. Consistent with this, it uses a quantitative / qualitative / mixed-methods, cross-sectional / longitudinal / experimental design. Section 3.1 … Section 3.4 …

Worksheet A1

Answer in your own words first, then compare with the model answer.

  1. Arrange methods, methodology and research design from the broadest to the narrowest, and explain in two sentences why the order matters when you write Chapter 3. (4 marks)

    Methodology, then research design, then methods. The order matters because each level must be justified by the one above it: the methods must suit the design, and the design must follow from the methodology. An examiner reading Chapter 3 looks for that chain of justification.

  2. Classify each item as methodology (M), research design (D) or method (T): (a) semi-structured interviews; (b) pragmatism; (c) explanatory sequential mixed methods; (d) the Krejcie–Morgan sample size; (e) a fixed-effects panel regression; (f) a quasi-experimental difference-in-differences design. (6 marks)

    (a) T, a data collection technique; (b) M, a paradigm; (c) D, an overall plan combining two strands; (d) T, part of the sampling method; (e) T, an estimation model; (f) D, the overall design, which is implemented through a method (the DiD regression). Accept (f) as T if the student justifies it as the estimator rather than the design.

  3. A student's draft Chapter 3 opens with three pages on positivism, interpretivism and critical realism, then moves straight to the sampling design. What would you advise? (4 marks)

    Cut the survey of paradigms to one or two sentences that name the position the study takes and show what it implies for this study's design. Three pages of general philosophy read like a textbook. Chapter 3 is not a tutorial, and paradigms not adopted are irrelevant. Then add a sentence linking the paradigm to the design before the sampling section begins.

  4. Which of these sentences belong in Chapter 3 and which in Chapter 2? (a) “Following Mincer (1974), earnings enter the model in logarithms.” (b) “Returns to schooling in Southeast Asia range from 6% to 12% (Author, Year).” (c) “The partial proportional odds model is used because the parallel-lines assumption is violated for two covariates (Williams, 2006).” (3 marks)

    (a) Chapter 3: it justifies a methodological choice. (b) Chapter 2: it is a substantive finding from the literature. (c) Chapter 3: it justifies the estimator with a methodological reference.

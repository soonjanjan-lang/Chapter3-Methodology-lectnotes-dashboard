F2Glossary

Short definitions of the terms used across this guide, with a pointer to the section that explains each one. Search (Ctrl K) finds them too.

ACDEFHILMNOPRSTV

A

Acquiescence bias
The tendency to agree with statements regardless of their content (E3).
Adapt (an instrument)
Modify an established instrument's wording, context, language or format; requires re-validation (E2).
Adopt (an instrument)
Use an established instrument unchanged (E2).
Attenuation bias
The shrinking of a regression coefficient towards zero caused by classical measurement error in a regressor (E4).
Average marginal effect (AME)
The average, over the sample, of the change in predicted probability for a one-unit change in a regressor; the usual way to report logit and probit results (B4).
Average variance extracted (AVE)
The mean squared standardised loading of a construct's items; ≥ 0.50 supports convergent validity (E4).

C

Cluster sampling
Selecting groups (villages, schools) and then units within them (D2).
Codebook
A table defining every variable's name, meaning, source item, level of measurement and coding (B3).
Common-method bias
Spurious correlation arising because all variables are measured with the same method at the same time (E4).
Composite reliability (CR)
A model-based reliability coefficient from standardised loadings, equal to omega for a single-factor model (E4).
Construct
The concept to be measured, often latent, such as trust or perceived usefulness (E1).
Content validity index (CVI)
The proportion of experts rating an item relevant; averaged across items for a scale-level index (E4).
Convenience sampling
Recruiting whoever is available and willing (D3).
Cronbach's alpha
A coefficient of internal-consistency reliability for a set of items (E4).
Cross-sectional data
Many units observed at one point in time (C2).

D

Design effect (DEFF)
The ratio of a complex design's variance to that of a simple random sample of the same size; \(1 + (m-1)\rho\) for equal clusters (D4).
Design weight
The inverse of a unit's selection probability, \(N_h/n_h\) in stratified sampling (D2).
Difference-in-differences (DiD)
Compares the change in outcomes for a treated group with the change for a comparison group (C2, B4).
Discriminant validity
Evidence that measures of different constructs are distinct (Fornell–Larcker, HTMT) (E4).
Dummy variable
A 0/1 variable named after the category coded 1; its mean is the share coded 1 (C1).

E

Effect size
The magnitude of a difference or association, such as Cohen's d or f², used in power analysis (D4).
Exclusion restriction
The assumption that an instrument affects the outcome only through the endogenous regressor (A2).
External validity
Whether findings apply beyond the study's setting and sample (E4).

F

Fixed effects
Unit-specific intercepts in panel data that absorb all time-invariant characteristics (C2).
Focus group discussion (FGD)
A moderated group discussion among 6–10 people sharing a relevant characteristic (C3).
Formative index
A composite whose indicators define the construct and need not correlate; alpha is inappropriate (E4).
Fornell–Larcker criterion
Discriminant validity holds if each construct's √AVE exceeds its correlations with other constructs (E4).

H

Hausman test
Tests whether fixed- and random-effects estimates differ systematically; rejection favours fixed effects (C2).
HTMT
Heterotrait–monotrait ratio of correlations; below 0.85 (or 0.90) supports discriminant validity (E4).

I

Identification strategy
The design feature that lets an estimate be read causally, together with its assumption (A2).
Instrument (data collection)
The tool used to collect data: questionnaire, interview guide, observation sheet (E1).
Internal validity
Whether an estimated relationship can credibly be read as causal (A2, E4).
Interval scale
Ordered values with equal intervals but no true zero (C1).

L

Likert scale
Strictly, several agreement items combined to measure one construct; a single item is a Likert-type item (E3).

M

Margin of error
Half the width of a confidence interval; ±5 percentage points in the Krejcie–Morgan table (D4).
Methodology
The philosophy and logic that justify how knowledge is produced; it contains research design, which contains methods (A1).
Methods
The specific techniques and tools of data collection and analysis (A1).

N

Nominal scale
Categories without order (C1).
Non-probability sampling
Designs in which selection probabilities are unknown or zero for some units (D3).

O

Omega (McDonald's ω)
A factor-model-based reliability coefficient that allows unequal loadings (E4).
Omitted variable bias
Bias from leaving out a variable that affects the outcome and is correlated with an included regressor (A2).
Operationalisation
Turning a concept into a measurable, coded variable (B3).
Ordinal scale
Ordered categories with unknown or unequal intervals (C1).

P

Panel data
The same units observed repeatedly over time (C2).
Paradigm
A set of assumptions about what exists and how it can be known: positivism, interpretivism, pragmatism (A1).
Parallel trends
The DiD assumption that treated and comparison groups would have moved together without treatment (A2).
Pilot study
A small trial of the instrument and procedures before fieldwork (E1).
Pooled cross-sections
Independent samples from the same population at different times (C2).
Power
The probability of detecting an effect of a given size when it exists; usually set at 0.80 (D4).
Probability sampling
Every unit has a known, non-zero chance of selection (D2).
Purposive sampling
Deliberately selecting units that meet criteria relevant to the research (D3).

R

Ratio scale
Equal intervals and a true zero (C1).
Reflective measurement
A model in which the latent construct causes its indicators, which should therefore correlate (E4).
Reliability
The consistency or stability of measurement (E4).
Research design
The overall plan of a study; sits between methodology and methods (A1).
Response rate
Usable responses divided by eligible units invited (B1).
RQ–method table
A table pairing each research question with its data, variables and method (B2).

S

Sampling frame
The list or procedure from which the sample is drawn (D1).
Saturation
The point at which new interviews add no new themes (C3).
Simple random sampling
Every unit, and every sample of size n, has an equal chance of selection (D2).
Snowball sampling
Participants recruit further participants; used for hidden populations (D3).
Stratified random sampling
Random sampling within non-overlapping subgroups (strata) (D2).
Systematic sampling
Selecting every k-th unit after a random start (D2).

T

Target population
The precisely defined population the findings describe (D1).
Test–retest reliability
Agreement between scores from two administrations of the same instrument (E4).
Time-series data
One unit observed over many periods (C2).

V

Validity
The degree to which evidence supports the intended interpretation of scores, and more broadly the soundness of conclusions (E4).
